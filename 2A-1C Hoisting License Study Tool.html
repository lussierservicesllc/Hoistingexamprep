MGL Chapter 146 & 230 CMR 6 2A-1C Scope

Chapter 146 is Massachusetts' Boiler, Pressure Vessel & Hoisting Machinery law. 230 CMR 6 is the Board of Boiler Rules' implementing regulation. Click any section to expand. Bookmark key facts to review later.

★ Bookmarked Facts
§ 53

License Required to Operate Hoisting Machinery

▼

Core Rule

No person shall operate any hoisting machinery in Massachusetts without a valid license issued by the Board of Boiler Rules unless expressly exempt. Must carry license on person and produce it on demand.

⚠️ NOTE ON CRANES: Crane operation is a separate license class with additional requirements beyond the scope of this study tool. This tool covers the 2A hoisting license and general hoisting equipment only. For crane licensing requirements contact the Board of Boiler Rules at 617-727-9640.

What You Can Operate with a 2A License

  • Excavators / backhoes
  • Bulldozers
  • Scrapers
  • Graders
  • Trenching machines
  • Hydraulic hammers / breakers
  • Skid steers over 10,000 lbs
  • Large loaders over 10,000 lbs
  • Draglines
  • Clamshells
  • Any hoisting machinery over 10,000 lbs not otherwise classified

License Classes Summary

  • 2A – Hoisting equipment >10,000 lb (this tool)
  • 2B – Hoisting up to 10,000 lb
  • 4D – Forklifts
  • Crane classes – Separate licensing — beyond scope of this tool

Exemptions

  • Elevator operators (separate statute)
  • Farmer on own land only
  • Residential garden/landscaping only
§ 53A

Board of Boiler Rules — Authority & Composition

▼

The Board of Boiler Rules (Dept. of Public Safety) administers hoisting licenses. It sets rules, holds hearings, issues/suspends/revokes licenses, and appoints Hoisting Inspectors.

§53A: Board of Boiler Rules issues licenses. Inspectors can stop work, cite violations, and order equipment out of service.

Inspectors

  • Appointed by the Board
  • Authority to stop work, issue citations, order equipment removed from service
  • Operator must comply immediately with a lawful inspector order
  • Fines up to $1,000 per day per violation
§ 54A

Examination & Licensing Requirements

▼

Eligibility

  • Must be at least 18 years old
  • Must pass written Board examination
  • Practical competency demonstration for some classes
License term = 3 years. Expired license = same as NO license.

Endorsements

  • 2A base + 1C endorsement required for crane operation
  • Each endorsement may require separate testing
  • Renewal: 3-year cycle, fee + continued compliance
§ 57

Accidents — Reporting Requirements

▼
§57: Report within 24 hours. BOTH operator AND owner/employer must report. Covers death, serious injury, OR substantial property damage.

Scene Preservation

  • Equipment must NOT be moved until released by proper authority
  • Tampering before release = separate criminal offense
  • Board Inspector investigates and releases hoisting equipment

Inspector's Role at Scene

  • May impound equipment and gather evidence
  • May prohibit further operation pending investigation
  • Failure to report = separate, additional violation
§ 65

Penalties & Violations

▼
§65: Operating without license = up to $1,000 fine AND/OR 1 year imprisonment PER DAY. Employer who knowingly hires unlicensed operator faces same penalties.

Common Exam Violations

  • Operating while impaired → automatic suspension
  • Refusing to submit to inspection
  • Failure to report accident within 24 hours
  • Moving equipment from accident scene without authorization
  • Operating beyond rated capacity
  • Felony conviction → possible automatic revocation
230 CMR 6

Hoisting Machinery Regulations — Key Rules

▼

Operator Duties (6.05)

  • Pre-shift inspection required before use — document defects
  • Tag out defective equipment immediately
  • Absolute right to refuse unsafe equipment — cannot be punished
  • Must know rated capacity at all times
230 CMR: Operator has absolute right to refuse unsafe equipment. Cannot be fired or disciplined.

Signalperson (6.05)

  • Required when operator can't see load or landing zone
  • Only ONE designated signalperson at a time
  • ANYONE may give STOP/Emergency Stop

Power Lines (6.08)

Minimum 10 feet clearance from energized lines up to 50kV. Add 1 inch per kV above 50kV.

Outriggers (6.06)

  • Fully extended and set before any lift unless load chart says otherwise
  • Assess ground conditions — mats/blocking on soft ground

Load Charts

  • Must be physically in the cab and legible — not memorized
  • Workers never allowed under a suspended load
  • Taglines required to control swinging loads

🚨 Accident Scene — Who Can Release It?

Equipment involved in a serious accident must NOT be moved until officially released. ALL applicable authorities must release before work resumes. The employer has zero authority here.

Board of Boiler Rules Inspector Can Release
Primary authority under MGL Ch. 146. Must investigate and formally release hoisting equipment before it moves. Most important for your exam.
State Police / Local Police Scene Control
Controls access to the scene for safety and criminal investigation. Their release of the scene ≠ equipment is cleared. Board Inspector must also clear the equipment specifically.
OSHA Inspector Federal Auth.
On federal projects or where OSHA jurisdiction applies, an OSHA officer may need to clear equipment too. Runs parallel to, not instead of, the Board of Boiler Rules.
Medical Examiner Fatality Only
In a fatal accident, the ME must be notified and may examine the scene before anything moves. Required for the scene — not specific to the equipment.
DA / State Police Detectives Criminal Inv.
If criminal liability is possible (manslaughter, negligence), detectives may hold scene jurisdiction. Their release may be required before work resumes.
Employer / Foreman / Owner ✗ NO
These parties cannot release the accident scene or clear equipment. An employer ordering a move before official release is committing a separate violation and exposing all parties to liability.
🔑 Exam Key Points — Accidents
  • Report to Board of Boiler Rules within 24 hours
  • Both operator AND owner/employer must independently report
  • Equipment must NOT be moved without Board Inspector release
  • Employer cannot authorize equipment to move
  • Moving without authorization = criminal offense + license risk
  • All applicable agencies (Board, OSHA, police) must release before resuming
  • Failure to report = separate criminal offense on top of the accident
  • Property-damage-only accidents still trigger 24-hour report requirement
🪝 Rigging — Rules & Requirements 230 CMR 6 / ASME B30

Rigging is the attachment of loads to hoisting equipment. Improper rigging is one of the leading causes of crane accidents. The 2A-1C exam heavily tests sling angles, inspection requirements, and load calculation.

SLINGS

Sling Types & Rating Rules

▼

Types of Slings

  • Wire rope slings — most common for heavy loads; inspect for broken wires, kinks, corrosion
  • Chain slings — high strength, heat resistant; inspect for stretched, bent, or cracked links
  • Synthetic web slings (nylon/polyester) — softer on loads; inspect for cuts, burns, UV damage
  • Roundslings — polyester core with protective cover; inspect for cuts and core exposure

Sling Rejection Criteria — Remove from Service If:

  • Wire rope: 10 broken wires in any rope lay, or 5 in one strand
  • Any kinking, crushing, bird-caging, or heat damage
  • Chain: any link stretched more than 3% of original length
  • Synthetic: any cut, burn, knot, or chemical damage
  • Missing or illegible tag/identification
EXAM KEY: A sling with no tag or an illegible tag must be removed from service immediately — you cannot use it even if it looks fine.

Sling Angle Effect on Capacity

As the angle between the sling and the horizontal decreases, the tension in each leg increases dramatically. This is the most math-heavy rigging concept on the exam.

  • 90° (vertical) — full rated capacity per leg
  • 60° — each leg carries about 115% of its share of the load
  • 45° — each leg carries about 141% of its share
  • 30° — each leg carries 200% — capacity is cut in half
Never rig at an angle less than 30°. Below 30°, the sling legs are bearing more than double the load share — catastrophic failure risk.
HITCHES

Hitch Configurations

▼
  • Vertical (straight) hitch — one sling, vertical, direct pull. Full rated capacity. Load can spin.
  • Choker hitch — sling loops around the load and chokes tight. Reduces rated capacity to 75%. Controls load spinning.
  • Basket hitch — sling under the load with both ends on the hook. Can double capacity if legs are vertical. Load can tip — use only on stable loads.
  • Double wrap choker — wraps twice for better grip. Capacity slightly higher than single choker.
Choker hitch = 75% of rated capacity. Always de-rate when using a choker. This is a very common exam question.

Hardware

  • Shackles — must be rated and pin must be moused (secured). Never replace a screw pin with a bolt.
  • Hook — must have a safety latch. Latch must be functional. Never force a load into the tip of the hook (point loading).
  • Rigging rings/links — must be rated for the load. No field-welded rigging hardware.
  • Softeners/padding required when sling contacts sharp edges
INSPECTION

Rigging Inspection Requirements

▼

Who Inspects

  • A qualified rigger must inspect all rigging before each use
  • Annual inspection by a qualified person required for all rigging hardware
  • Operator must visually verify rigging before the lift

Pre-Lift Check

  • Verify sling rating vs. load weight
  • Check sling angle — calculate actual load on each leg
  • Confirm all shackle pins are seated and moused
  • Verify hook latch is functional
  • Ensure load is balanced — center of gravity below the hook
  • Check that taglines are attached and handlers are clear
The operator has the right and obligation to refuse a lift if rigging appears unsafe or is not rated for the load. This cannot be overridden by the employer.

Load Weight — Know Before You Lift

  • Weight must be known or calculated before rigging
  • Steel: approximately 490 lbs per cubic foot
  • Concrete: approximately 150 lbs per cubic foot
  • Load must never exceed the rated capacity of the rigging OR the crane's load chart for that configuration
TAG LINES

Taglines & Load Control

▼
  • Taglines are required on all suspended loads to prevent uncontrolled spinning or swinging
  • Must be long enough to be controlled from a safe distance
  • Tagline handlers must stand clear of the load's fall zone
  • Never wrap a tagline around your hand or body
  • Non-conductive taglines required near energized power lines
Never stand under or in the swing path of a suspended load — regardless of taglines. Taglines guide the load; they don't make it safe to be underneath it.
⛏️ Excavation, Trenching & Shoring 520 CMR / OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P

Excavation and trenching is one of the most dangerous construction operations. Massachusetts follows OSHA Subpart P for excavation safety. The 2A-1C exam tests soil classification, protective systems, and safe entry/exit requirements.

DEFINITIONS

Excavation vs. Trench — Key Definitions

▼
  • Excavation — any man-made cut, cavity, trench, or depression in the earth's surface formed by earth removal
  • Trench — a narrow excavation (in relation to its length) made below the surface of the ground. Depth generally greater than its width, and width no greater than 15 feet
  • Competent Person — someone capable of identifying existing and predictable hazards and authorized to take corrective action. Required on site for ALL excavations.
A Competent Person must be on site for every excavation. This is non-negotiable under OSHA and Massachusetts law. The operator cannot substitute for this role unless specifically trained and designated.
SOIL TYPES

Soil Classification — Types A, B, C

▼

Type A — Most Stable

  • Cohesive soils: clay, silty clay, sandy clay
  • Unconfined compressive strength ≥ 1.5 tons/sq ft
  • No fissures, no vibration, not previously disturbed
  • Maximum allowable slope: 3/4:1 (53°)

Type B — Moderate Stability

  • Cohesive soil with fissures, or granular cohesionless soil
  • Unconfined compressive strength 0.5–1.5 tons/sq ft
  • Previously disturbed soils, or near sources of vibration
  • Maximum allowable slope: 1:1 (45°)

Type C — Least Stable

  • Granular soils: gravel, sand, loamy sand
  • Submerged soil, or soil from which water is freely seeping
  • Unconfined compressive strength less than 0.5 tons/sq ft
  • Maximum allowable slope: 1½:1 (34°)
Type C is the most common soil classification on exam questions. When in doubt, classify as Type C — it's the most conservative (safest) choice.
PROTECTIVE SYSTEMS

Sloping, Shoring & Trench Boxes

▼

When Required

  • Any excavation 5 feet or deeper requires a protective system unless solid rock
  • Competent Person may determine a protective system is needed at less than 5 feet if conditions warrant
  • Any excavation 20 feet or deeper requires a protective system designed by a registered Professional Engineer
5 feet = protective system required. 20 feet = PE design required. These are the two critical depth numbers.

Sloping

  • Cutting back trench walls to a stable angle based on soil type (see Type A/B/C above)
  • Simple, low cost — requires more surface area
  • Spoil pile must be kept at least 2 feet from the edge of the excavation

Shoring

  • Installing support structures (wood, aluminum, hydraulic) against trench walls
  • Installed from the top down, removed from the bottom up
  • Workers must never be in the trench while shoring is being installed or removed unless protected
  • Hydraulic shoring must be rated for the soil load

Trench Boxes (Shield Systems)

  • Pre-fabricated steel or aluminum box placed in the trench — workers work inside the box
  • Protects workers inside — does NOT protect walls outside the box
  • Must be rated for the soil conditions
  • Box must extend at least 18 inches above the top of the trench if used in a sloped excavation
ACCESS & EGRESS

Entry, Exit & Safety Requirements

▼
  • Ladders, steps, or ramps required for excavations 4 feet or deeper
  • Access point must be within 25 lateral feet of workers at all times
  • Ladders must extend at least 3 feet above the top of the excavation
  • Workers must not cross an excavation without a bridge or walkway with guardrails
4 feet = ladder required. 25 feet = max distance to the ladder. 3 feet = ladder must extend above the top. These three numbers appear on the exam.

Water Accumulation

  • Workers must not work in excavations where water has accumulated unless adequate precautions are taken
  • Water must be controlled (pumped out) and Competent Person must inspect after any rain event
  • Excavations in Type C soil near water are extremely high risk

Atmosphere Testing

  • Excavations deeper than 4 feet must be tested for hazardous atmospheres if conditions could create one (near fuel tanks, utilities, landfills)
  • Oxygen level must be 19.5%–23.5%
  • If atmosphere is hazardous, workers must not enter until it is safe

Inspection Requirements

  • Competent Person must inspect the excavation daily before workers enter, and after any rain, freeze/thaw, or other event that could affect stability
  • Spoil piles and equipment must stay at least 2 feet from the edge
  • No excavation equipment should be operated within the angle of repose of the trench wall without engineering analysis
DIGSAFE + EXCAVATION

DigSafe & Utility Clearance During Excavation

▼
  • Call 811 at least 72 hours before ANY excavation (excluding weekends/holidays)
  • Utility marks are valid for 30 days — re-notify if work continues past 30 days
  • Within 18 inches of a marked utility, excavation must be done by hand tools only — no mechanical equipment
  • If you expose an unmarked utility, stop work and notify the utility company
  • If you strike a gas line: evacuate immediately, call 911 and the gas company
18-inch hand-dig zone around marked utilities. This is a Massachusetts-specific rule that appears on the exam. No mechanical equipment within 18 inches of a marked utility line.
🚜 Forklifts (Powered Industrial Trucks) 4D License / OSHA 29 CFR 1910.178

In Massachusetts, operating a forklift requires a 4D hoisting license in addition to OSHA forklift operator training. The exam tests stability, load handling, pre-operation inspection, and pedestrian safety.

LICENSING

Massachusetts 4D License Requirements

▼
  • A 4D hoisting license is required to operate any forklift (powered industrial truck) in Massachusetts
  • This is separate from and in addition to OSHA operator training required by federal law
  • Must be at least 18 years old
  • License must be carried on person while operating
  • Employer must also ensure OSHA-compliant operator training is completed every 3 years or when unsafe operation is observed
Massachusetts requires BOTH a 4D state hoisting license AND OSHA operator training. Having one without the other is a violation.
STABILITY

Stability Triangle & Load Capacity

▼

Stability Triangle

A forklift's stability is based on a three-point system — two front drive wheels and the rear axle pivot point form a triangle. The forklift will tip over if the combined center of gravity (forklift + load) moves outside this triangle.

  • Adding a load shifts the center of gravity forward
  • Raising forks raises the combined center of gravity — increases tip-over risk
  • Turning with a raised load dramatically increases tip risk
  • Never turn on a ramp or incline with a raised load
The stability triangle is the most tested concept in forklift safety. The forklift tips when the center of gravity leaves the triangle — caused by overloading, raising forks too high, or turning too sharply.

Load Capacity & Data Plate

  • Every forklift must have a data plate (capacity plate) permanently attached and legible
  • Rated capacity is based on a standard load center (typically 24 inches from the face of the forks)
  • If the load center is farther than rated (long or off-center load), capacity is reduced
  • Never exceed the rated capacity — and derate for attachments (attachments reduce capacity)
  • Never remove or alter the data plate
PRE-OP

Pre-Operation Inspection

▼

A pre-shift inspection is required before every use. If a defect is found, the forklift must be tagged out and removed from service until repaired.

Check Before Starting

  • Fluid levels: engine oil, hydraulic fluid, coolant, fuel/battery charge
  • Tires: condition, inflation (pneumatic), no damage
  • Forks: cracks, bends, heel wear — fork heel thickness must not be worn more than 10%
  • Mast: chains, rollers, hydraulic cylinders — no leaks
  • Overhead guard: in place and undamaged
  • Data plate: present and legible
  • Seatbelt: functional
  • Horn, lights, warning devices: working
  • Brakes: tested at slow speed
Fork heel wear exceeding 10% = remove from service. The overhead guard must always be in place — it is never optional.
OPERATION

Safe Operating Rules

▼

Travel

  • Forks must be 4–6 inches off the ground when traveling
  • Mast must be tilted back when traveling with a load
  • Travel with load upgrade on ramps; travel unloaded downgrade (forks facing downhill)
  • Never exceed posted speed limits — generally 5 mph in pedestrian areas
  • Slow down and sound horn at blind intersections and doorways

Pedestrian Safety

  • Pedestrians always have the right of way
  • Maintain a minimum 3-foot clearance when passing pedestrians
  • Never allow passengers on a forklift unless a designated seat is provided
  • Never use forks to elevate a person unless an approved work platform with guardrails is attached

Fueling / Charging

  • Propane: engine off, no smoking, outdoors or well-ventilated area
  • Electric: charge in designated area, ventilate hydrogen gas, no sparks near batteries
  • Never fuel near open flames or sparks
Forks 4–6 inches off the ground while traveling. Mast tilted back. Load uphill on ramps. These three travel rules appear together on the exam.

Parking

  • Forks must be fully lowered to the ground when parked
  • Mast tilted forward slightly to rest forks flat
  • Engine off, parking brake set, key removed
  • Never park on an incline unless wheels are chocked
  • Never park blocking fire exits, emergency equipment, or fire suppression systems
REFUELING / BATTERY

Refueling, Recharging & Indoor Use

▼
  • LP gas, gasoline, and diesel forklifts produce carbon monoxide (CO) — must not be used indoors without adequate ventilation
  • Electric forklifts produce no emissions — preferred for indoor use
  • Propane cylinders must be inspected for leaks and damage before use
  • Battery charging produces hydrogen gas — explosion risk. No sparks or open flame in charging area.
  • Spilled battery acid must be neutralized and cleaned up immediately
Carbon monoxide poisoning from propane/gas forklifts indoors is a leading cause of forklift-related illness. Adequate ventilation or electric forklifts are required for indoor use.
