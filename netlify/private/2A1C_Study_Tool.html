MGL Chapter 146 & 230 CMR 6 2A-1C Scope

Chapter 146 is Massachusetts' boiler, pressure vessel & hoisting machinery law. 230 CMR 6.00 is the hoisting regulation, enforced by the Office of Public Safety and Inspections (OPSI) within the Division of Occupational Licensure. It replaced the old 520 CMR 6.00 and the former Board of Boiler Rules. Click any section to expand. Bookmark key facts to review later.

★ Bookmarked Facts
§ 53 / 53A

License Required to Operate Hoisting Machinery

▼

Core Rule

  • No person may operate hoisting machinery in Massachusetts without a valid license issued under M.G.L. c.146 §§53 and 53A and 230 CMR 6.
  • Licensing is handled by the Office of Public Safety and Inspections (OPSI), Division of Occupational Licensure. Phone (617) 727-3200.
  • The operator must carry the license (and a government ID) and show it on request (230 CMR 6.08).
  • Exemptions exist for some operations, for example exclusively agricultural work, utilities, company-owned equipment used on non-public property with in-service training, vocational schools and apprentice training facilities (c.146 §53; 230 CMR 6.06). Check the exact wording before relying on one.
An expired license is not a valid license. Carry it on your person. Licensing authority: OPSI.
230 CMR 6.10

License Classes

▼

Classes and What They Cover

  • 1A – Cranes: lattice boom, friction clutch, tower and similar cranes
  • 1B – Telescoping boom cranes with wire rope (a 1B holder may also operate 1C, 1D, 4B, 4C, 4E, 4F equipment as listed in the regulation)
  • 1C – Hydraulic telescoping boom cranes without wire rope (also covers 1D)
  • 1D – General industrial and warehouse forklifts, primarily used indoors
  • 2A – Crawler and rubber-tired excavators and backhoes (also covers 2B, 2C, 2D)
  • 2B – Loader/backhoe combinations
  • 2C – Front-end loaders
  • 2D – Compact hoisting machinery under 10,000 lbs GVW
  • 3A – Overhead gantry, bridge and similar cranes
  • 4B – Drill rigs and pile drivers
  • 4D – Concrete pumps
2A = excavators & backhoes. 1D = warehouse forklifts. 2D = compact machinery under 10,000 lbs GVW.

The class list is summarized. Check 230 CMR 6.10 for the full text, including which classes a higher class covers.

230 CMR 6.02 / 6.04

Exam, Age & License Term

▼

Eligibility and Exam

  • Minimum age 18. Apprentice licensees must be at least 16.
  • Written exam: minimum passing grade 70%.
  • Only one application per license class in any 60-day period.
  • Practical demonstration may be required for some classes.

Term & Renewal

  • Licenses renew every 2 years, on the licensee's birthday (even or odd year).
  • Renewal requires 2 classroom hours of continuing education per cycle for a single-class license: 1 hour of regulatory/industry-standard training and 1 hour of equipment-specific training.
  • A lapsed license is void. After it has been lapsed for more than a year you must take the exam again.
Age 18 (apprentice 16). Pass the written exam at 70%. Renew every 2 years with 2 hours of CE.
§ 54A

Penalties

▼

Fines & Jail

  • Operating without a valid license (violating §§53, 53A or 54): fine of $500 to $3,000 and/or up to 3 months in jail.
  • Knowingly permitting an unlicensed person to operate: fine of $1,000 to $3,000 and/or up to 3 months.
  • Obstructing an inspector: fine of $250 to $3,000.
§54A: operating unlicensed = $500–$3,000 and/or up to 3 months. Permitting an unlicensed operator = $1,000–$3,000 and/or up to 3 months.
§ 65

License Application, Exam & Revocation

▼
  • Sets out the license application and examination process.
  • A person whose license was revoked, or who has been penalized under §54A, must pass both the written and the practical exam to be licensed again.
  • The Office may also take licensing action under 230 CMR 6.12 (discipline). Do not assume any particular event causes an automatic suspension; the Office decides.
230 CMR 6.11

Accidents & Reporting

▼
  • Notify the OPSI accident hotline, (508) 820-1444, within 1 hour of a reportable incident.
  • Submit the written Injury and Incident Documentation within 48 hours.
  • Reportable: death, serious injury, or property damage over $10,000.
  • The report is made by the operating licensee, or the owner or owner's representative.
  • Do not move or dismantle the machinery until the Office or an Inspector has investigated, except to save life or property, remove injured persons, or allow emergency vehicles access.
Hotline within 1 hour. Written report within 48 hours. Property damage over $10,000 counts. Don't move the machine until an Inspector approves.
230 CMR 6.08

Operating Rules

▼

Operator Duties

  • Complete and document a daily inspection before use (6.08(2)(b)); keep the signed record with the machine.
  • Do not operate defective equipment; have it repaired first.
  • Know the rated capacity. The load chart must be legible and in the cab (6.09). Never exceed rated capacity; never use the LMI override key.
  • The operator has authority to stop and refuse to handle loads until safety has been assured (6.08(2)(c)(11)).
  • Carry your license and a government ID (6.08(2)(c)(20)).

Signal Person

  • Required when the operator cannot see the load or landing zone.
  • The operator responds only to the designated signal person, except that anyone may give a STOP (6.08(2)(c)(9)).
  • Having just one signal person at a time is standard industry practice; if signals are unclear, stop.

Power Lines

Stay at least 10 feet from energized lines up to 50 kV. Higher voltages need more: 15 ft (over 50–200 kV), 20 ft (over 200–350 kV), 25 ft (over 350–500 kV), 35 ft (over 500–750 kV), 45 ft (over 750–1,000 kV). Source: OSHA 1926.1408 Table A; 230 CMR 6.08(2)(c)(16) requires following the power-line rules.

Outriggers, Loads & Dig Safe

  • Use and set outriggers as the manufacturer requires (6.08(2)(c)(17)); the load chart gives capacity for each configuration.
  • No one under a suspended load (6.08(2)(c)(14)).
  • Comply with Dig Safe before excavating (6.08(2)(c)(18)).
  • Taglines are recommended to control loads and are required for personnel platforms; use non-conductive taglines near power lines.

🚨 Accidents — Reporting & Scene 230 CMR 6.11

After a reportable accident, call the OPSI hotline within 1 hour, send the written report within 48 hours, and do not move or dismantle the machinery until the Office or an Inspector has investigated. The employer cannot approve moving it.

OPSI Inspector Approves Move
The Office of Public Safety and Inspections investigates and approves before the machinery is moved or dismantled. Accident hotline: (508) 820-1444. Most important for your exam.
Operating Licensee Reporter
The operating licensee, or the owner or owner's representative, must make the report. Make sure it is actually made.
Police / Fire / EMS Scene Control
Emergency responders control the scene for safety. Their involvement does not replace the OPSI notice or Inspector approval.
OSHA Separate
OSHA has its own reporting rules (for example, fatalities and hospitalizations). These are separate from the OPSI requirements.
Employer / Foreman / Owner ✗ NO
These parties cannot approve moving the machinery. Only the Office or an Inspector can, once they have investigated.

🪝 Rigging — Rules & Requirements OSHA 1926 / ASME B30.9

Rigging is the attachment of loads to hoisting equipment. Improper rigging is one of the leading causes of crane accidents. The 2A-1C exam heavily tests sling angles, inspection requirements, and load calculation.

SLINGS

Sling Types & Rating Rules

▼

Types of Slings

  • Wire rope slings — most common for heavy loads; inspect for broken wires, kinks, corrosion
  • Chain slings — high strength, heat resistant; inspect for stretched, bent, or cracked links
  • Synthetic web slings (nylon/polyester) — softer on loads; inspect for cuts, burns, UV damage
  • Roundslings — polyester core with protective cover; inspect for cuts and core exposure

Sling Rejection Criteria — Remove from Service If:

  • Wire rope: 10 randomly distributed broken wires in one rope lay, or 5 in one strand (ASME B30.9)
  • Any kinking, crushing, bird-caging, or heat damage
  • Chain: any stretched, bent, nicked or gouged link, or wear beyond the manufacturer/ASME limits
  • Synthetic: any cut, burn, knot, or chemical damage
  • Missing or illegible tag/identification
EXAM KEY: A sling with no tag or an illegible tag must be removed from service immediately — you cannot use it even if it looks fine.

Sling Angle Effect on Capacity

As the angle between the sling and the horizontal decreases, the tension in each leg increases dramatically. This is the most math-heavy rigging concept on the exam.

  • 90° (vertical) — full rated capacity per leg
  • 60° — each leg carries about 115% of its share of the load
  • 45° — each leg carries about 141% of its share
  • 30° — each leg carries 200% — capacity is cut in half
Never rig at an angle less than 30°. Below 30°, the sling legs are bearing more than double the load share — catastrophic failure risk.
HITCHES

Hitch Configurations

▼
  • Vertical (straight) hitch — one sling, vertical, direct pull. Full rated capacity. Load can spin.
  • Choker hitch — sling loops around the load and chokes tight. Rated capacity is lower than a vertical hitch and depends on the hitch and angle; use the capacity marked on the sling tag. Controls load spinning.
  • Basket hitch — sling under the load with both ends on the hook. Can double capacity if legs are vertical. Load can tip — use only on stable loads.
  • Double wrap choker — wraps twice for better grip. Capacity slightly higher than single choker.
Choker hitch: always use the rated capacity marked on the tag for that hitch and angle. It is lower than a vertical hitch.

Hardware

  • Shackles — must be rated. A round-pin shackle pin must be secured (moused or cotter-pinned). Never replace a screw pin with a bolt.
  • Hook — must have a safety latch. Latch must be functional. Never force a load into the tip of the hook (point loading).
  • Rigging rings/links — must be rated for the load. No field-welded rigging hardware.
  • Softeners/padding required when sling contacts sharp edges
INSPECTION

Rigging Inspection Requirements

▼

Who Inspects

  • A competent person must inspect slings and rigging each day before use (OSHA 1926.251(a)(6))
  • Periodic inspections are also required (for example, alloy steel chain at least every 12 months, with records)
  • Operator must visually verify rigging before the lift

Pre-Lift Check

  • Verify sling rating vs. load weight
  • Check sling angle — calculate actual load on each leg
  • Confirm all shackle pins are seated and secured
  • Verify hook latch is functional
  • Ensure load is balanced — center of gravity below the hook
  • Check that taglines are attached and handlers are clear
The operator has the right and obligation to refuse a lift if rigging appears unsafe or is not rated for the load. This cannot be overridden by the employer.

Load Weight — Know Before You Lift

  • Weight must be known or calculated before rigging
  • Steel: approximately 490 lbs per cubic foot
  • Concrete: approximately 150 lbs per cubic foot
  • Load must never exceed the rated capacity of the rigging OR the crane's load chart for that configuration
TAG LINES

Taglines & Load Control

▼
  • Taglines are recommended to control spinning or swinging, and are required for personnel platforms (230 CMR 6.09)
  • Must be long enough to be controlled from a safe distance
  • Tagline handlers must stand clear of the load's fall zone
  • Never wrap a tagline around your hand or body
  • Non-conductive taglines required near energized power lines
Never stand under or in the swing path of a suspended load — regardless of taglines. Taglines guide the load; they don't make it safe to be underneath it.
⛏️ Excavation, Trenching & Shoring OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P

Excavation and trenching is one of the most dangerous construction operations. Excavations follow OSHA Subpart P. Massachusetts and local authorities may add their own trench requirements or permits, so check before work. The 2A-1C exam tests soil classification, protective systems, and safe entry/exit requirements.

DEFINITIONS

Excavation vs. Trench — Key Definitions

▼
  • Excavation — any man-made cut, cavity, trench, or depression in the earth's surface formed by earth removal
  • Trench — a narrow excavation (in relation to its length) made below the surface of the ground. Depth generally greater than its width, and width no greater than 15 feet
  • Competent Person — someone capable of identifying existing and predictable hazards and authorized to take corrective action. OSHA requires a competent person to inspect excavations daily and as conditions change.
A Competent Person must inspect the excavation daily before work and after anything that could change conditions. They need authority to remove workers and correct hazards. An operator can only fill the role if trained and designated.
SOIL TYPES

Soil Classification — Types A, B, C

▼

Type A — Most Stable

  • Cohesive soils: clay, silty clay, sandy clay
  • Unconfined compressive strength ≥ 1.5 tons/sq ft
  • No fissures, no vibration, not previously disturbed
  • Maximum allowable slope: 3/4:1 (53°)

Type B — Moderate Stability

  • Cohesive soil with fissures, or granular cohesionless soil
  • Unconfined compressive strength 0.5–1.5 tons/sq ft
  • Previously disturbed soils, or near sources of vibration
  • Maximum allowable slope: 1:1 (45°)

Type C — Least Stable

  • Granular soils: gravel, sand, loamy sand
  • Submerged soil, or soil from which water is freely seeping
  • Unconfined compressive strength less than 0.5 tons/sq ft
  • Maximum allowable slope: 1½:1 (34°)
Type C is the most common soil classification on exam questions. When in doubt, classify as Type C — it's the most conservative (safest) choice.
PROTECTIVE SYSTEMS

Sloping, Shoring & Trench Boxes

▼

When Required

  • Any excavation 5 feet or deeper requires a protective system unless solid rock
  • Competent Person may determine a protective system is needed at less than 5 feet if conditions warrant
  • Any excavation 20 feet or deeper requires a protective system designed by a registered Professional Engineer
5 feet = protective system required. 20 feet = PE design required. These are the two critical depth numbers.

Sloping

  • Cutting back trench walls to a stable angle based on soil type (see Type A/B/C above)
  • Simple, low cost — requires more surface area
  • Spoil pile must be kept at least 2 feet from the edge of the excavation

Shoring

  • Installing support structures (wood, aluminum, hydraulic) against trench walls
  • Installed from the top down, removed from the bottom up
  • Workers must never be in the trench while shoring is being installed or removed unless protected
  • Hydraulic shoring must be rated for the soil load

Trench Boxes (Shield Systems)

  • Pre-fabricated steel or aluminum box placed in the trench — workers work inside the box
  • Protects workers inside — does NOT protect walls outside the box
  • Must be rated for the soil conditions
  • Installed and used per the manufacturer's tabulated data; the excavation may go no more than 2 feet below the bottom of the shield (OSHA 1926.652(g)(1)(iii))
ACCESS & EGRESS

Entry, Exit & Safety Requirements

▼
  • Ladders, steps, or ramps required for excavations 4 feet or deeper
  • Access point must be within 25 lateral feet of workers at all times
  • Ladders must extend at least 3 feet above the top of the excavation
  • Workers must not cross an excavation without a bridge or walkway with guardrails
4 feet = ladder required. 25 feet = max distance to the ladder. 3 feet = ladder must extend above the top. These three numbers appear on the exam.

Water Accumulation

  • Workers must not work in excavations where water has accumulated unless adequate precautions are taken
  • Water must be controlled (pumped out) and Competent Person must inspect after any rain event
  • Excavations in Type C soil near water are extremely high risk

Atmosphere Testing

  • Excavations deeper than 4 feet must be tested for hazardous atmospheres if conditions could create one (near fuel tanks, utilities, landfills)
  • Atmospheres with less than 19.5% oxygen are oxygen-deficient: do not enter
  • If atmosphere is hazardous, workers must not enter until it is safe

Inspection Requirements

  • Competent Person must inspect the excavation daily before workers enter, and after any rain, freeze/thaw, or other event that could affect stability
  • Spoil piles and equipment must stay at least 2 feet from the edge
  • No excavation equipment should be operated within the angle of repose of the trench wall without engineering analysis
DIGSAFE + EXCAVATION

DigSafe & Utility Clearance During Excavation

▼
  • Call 811 at least 72 hours before excavation (excluding Saturdays, Sundays and holidays)
  • Your Dig Safe ticket is valid for 30 calendar days — re-notify if work continues past that
  • Marked facilities have an 18-inch safety zone on each side. Use nonmechanical means as necessary near them; mechanical equipment only for the initial penetration of pavement or rock (220 CMR 99.07)
  • If you find an unmarked or mismarked facility, stop work and notify the Dig Safe Center (220 CMR 99.07(6))
  • If you strike a gas line: evacuate immediately, call 911 and notify the gas company
Safety zone = facility width plus 18 inches each side (220 CMR 99.06(2)). Dig by nonmechanical means as necessary to avoid damage.
🚜 Forklifts (Powered Industrial Trucks) 1D License / OSHA 29 CFR 1910.178

In Massachusetts, a general industrial or warehouse forklift, used primarily indoors, falls under the 1D hoisting license, in addition to OSHA forklift operator training (some company-owned, non-public-property operations are exempt). The exam tests stability, load handling, pre-operation inspection, and pedestrian safety.

LICENSING

Massachusetts 1D License Requirements

▼
  • A 1D hoisting license covers general industrial and warehouse forklifts primarily used indoors (230 CMR 6.10). Exemptions apply to some company-owned equipment on non-public property (c.146 §53; 230 CMR 6.06)
  • This is separate from and in addition to OSHA operator training required by federal law
  • Must be at least 18 years old
  • License must be carried on person while operating
  • Employer must also ensure OSHA-compliant operator training is completed every 3 years or when unsafe operation is observed
Where a state license is required, you need it in addition to OSHA operator training. Employer evaluation of each operator is required at least every 3 years.
STABILITY

Stability Triangle & Load Capacity

▼

Stability Triangle

A forklift's stability is based on a three-point system — two front drive wheels and the rear axle pivot point form a triangle. The forklift will tip over if the combined center of gravity (forklift + load) moves outside this triangle.

  • Adding a load shifts the center of gravity forward
  • Raising forks raises the combined center of gravity — increases tip-over risk
  • Turning with a raised load dramatically increases tip risk
  • Never turn on a ramp or incline with a raised load
The stability triangle is the most tested concept in forklift safety. The forklift tips when the center of gravity leaves the triangle — caused by overloading, raising forks too high, or turning too sharply.

Load Capacity & Data Plate

  • Every forklift must have a data plate (capacity plate) permanently attached and legible
  • Rated capacity is based on a standard load center (typically 24 inches from the face of the forks)
  • If the load center is farther than rated (long or off-center load), capacity is reduced
  • Never exceed the rated capacity — and derate for attachments (attachments reduce capacity)
  • Never remove or alter the data plate
PRE-OP

Pre-Operation Inspection

▼

A pre-shift inspection is required before every use. If a defect is found, the forklift must be tagged out and removed from service until repaired.

Check Before Starting

  • Fluid levels: engine oil, hydraulic fluid, coolant, fuel/battery charge
  • Tires: condition, inflation (pneumatic), no damage
  • Forks: cracks, bends, heel wear — fork wear must stay within the manufacturer's limits (industry guidance: replace forks worn more than 10% at the heel)
  • Mast: chains, rollers, hydraulic cylinders — no leaks
  • Overhead guard: in place and undamaged
  • Data plate: present and legible
  • Seatbelt: functional
  • Horn, lights, warning devices: working
  • Brakes: tested at slow speed
Forks worn beyond the manufacturer limit (industry guidance: 10% at the heel) are removed from service. Keep the overhead guard in place.
OPERATION

Safe Operating Rules

▼

Travel

  • Forks should be carried low when traveling (industry guidance: about 4–6 inches)
  • Mast must be tilted back when traveling with a load
  • Travel with load upgrade on ramps; travel unloaded downgrade (forks facing downhill)
  • Never exceed posted speed limits; travel at a speed that lets you stop safely (many sites use about 5 mph)
  • Slow down and sound horn at blind intersections and doorways

Pedestrian Safety

  • Yield to pedestrians (site rules usually give them the right of way)
  • Keep a safe distance when passing pedestrians
  • Never allow riders unless a safe place to ride is provided
  • Never use forks to elevate a person unless an approved work platform with guardrails is attached

Fueling / Charging

  • Propane: engine off, no smoking, outdoors or well-ventilated area
  • Electric: charge in designated area, ventilate hydrogen gas, no sparks near batteries
  • Never fuel near open flames or sparks
Forks low while traveling. Mast tilted back. Load uphill on ramps. These three travel rules appear together.

Parking

  • Forks must be fully lowered to the ground when parked
  • Mast tilted forward slightly to rest forks flat
  • Engine off, parking brake set, key removed
  • Never park on an incline unless wheels are chocked
  • Never park blocking fire exits, emergency equipment, or fire suppression systems
REFUELING / BATTERY

Refueling, Recharging & Indoor Use

▼
  • LP gas, gasoline, and diesel forklifts produce carbon monoxide (CO) — must not be used indoors without adequate ventilation
  • Electric forklifts produce no emissions — preferred for indoor use
  • Propane cylinders must be inspected for leaks and damage before use
  • Battery charging produces hydrogen gas — explosion risk. No sparks or open flame in charging area.
  • Spilled battery acid must be neutralized and cleaned up immediately
Carbon monoxide poisoning from propane/gas forklifts indoors is a leading cause of forklift-related illness. Adequate ventilation or electric forklifts are required for indoor use.
